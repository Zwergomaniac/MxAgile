# MxMocketeer Discovery Assessment Guide

## Purpose
This file governs how MxMocketeer assesses a mockup's maturity, detects gaps,
runs adaptive interviews, and determines readiness. Detailed mechanics live here;
the system prompt contains only mandatory behavioral invariants.

---

## Two Readiness Concepts

PROTOTYPE_READINESS
  Enough is understood to create and usefully discuss the current prototype.
  Threshold: intentionally low — early visualization is valuable.

DEVELOPMENT_HANDOFF_READINESS
  The design is sufficiently explored and explicit for professional development/refinement.
  Threshold: materially higher — critical dimensions sufficient, no blocking gaps.

A prototype may legitimately be PROTOTYPE_READY but not HANDOFF_READY.
This is the normal, expected state for an early mockup. Never treat it as an error.

---

## When to Assess

- After any meaningful change (new requirements, role clarification, rule added)
- When explicitly requested ("Are we ready for development?")
- When the user asks for a maturity summary
- When generating handoff guidance

Do NOT require full assessment before producing the first mockup.
Assessment enriches the mockup; it does not gate it.

---

## Assessment Procedure

Step 1 — INSPECT CURRENT STATE
  Read the complete embedded Design Contract.
  Identify what is present, what is confirmed, what is uncertain.

Step 2 — EVALUATE EACH DIMENSION
  For each standard dimension (see Design Contract schema):
  - What evidence exists? (confirmed, derived, assumption, open, missing)
  - What gaps remain?
  - Is this dimension UNEXPLORED / PARTIAL / SUFFICIENT / STRONG / BLOCKED / CONFLICTING?

Step 3 — IDENTIFY AND CLASSIFY GAPS
  For each gap:
  - Assign stable GAP-NNN id
  - Classify by category
  - Assess priority (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) based on:
      * security/privacy risk
      * business/financial impact
      * implementation cost if misunderstood
      * number of downstream decisions affected
      * blocking vs. non-blocking character
  - Set blocking: true only when it materially prevents handoff or development

Step 4 — DETERMINE READINESS
  PROTOTYPE_READY: yes, unless goal/users completely unknown
  HANDOFF_READY: requires all HANDOFF_READINESS_REQUIREMENTS below

Step 5 — UPDATE CONTRACT
  Write assessment block to the Design Contract.
  Do not increment revision solely for an assessment update — increment only
  when design content (screens, requirements, roles, rules) changes.

---

## Handoff Readiness Requirements

All of the following must be SUFFICIENT or STRONG; none may be BLOCKED or CONFLICTING:

1. DIM-PURPOSE_SCOPE — goal, actors, MVP scope clear
2. DIM-FUNCTIONAL_COVERAGE — critical journeys navigable, at least one end-to-end flow
3. DIM-BUSINESS_RULES — critical calculations and rules explicit (not UNKNOWN)
4. DIM-ROLES_CAPABILITIES — roles defined with can/cannot; data scope not UNKNOWN for critical roles
5. DIM-DATA_SEMANTICS — key data objects and their meaning clear
6. DIM-SECURITY_PRIVACY — access rules clear where security-relevant; no SECURITY_UNCLEAR blocker
7. DIM-VALIDATION_ERROR_HANDLING — required fields and critical error paths defined
8. No blocking gaps (blocking: true) remain OPEN
9. No unresolved CONFLICT items that affect critical business decisions
10. Assumptions with material downstream risk are explicitly visible

The following dimensions may be PARTIAL or UNEXPLORED without blocking handoff
(unless they contain a high-priority blocking gap):
  DIM-ACCESSIBILITY, DIM-NON_FUNCTIONAL, DIM-UX_NAVIGATION (minor aesthetic detail),
  DIM-BUSINESS_FLOWS (structured flows are valuable but not mandatory for initial handoff;
  PARTIAL acceptable when critical journeys are at least navigable in the mockup)

---

## Assessment Is Not the Interview

Assessment determines what is unknown.
The Interview asks the right questions to reduce uncertainty.
Keep these phases separate.

---

## Adaptive Interview Strategy

When to interview:
  After assessment identifies gaps that warrant clarification.
  When the user requests questions or asks to "continue discovery."

Question selection:
  Select the highest-priority unresolved gaps.
  For each gap, formulate ONE clear, understandable question.
  Batch 3–6 questions at a time (context-dependent — fewer for simple topics,
  up to 6 for complex multi-role scenarios).

Question quality rules:
  - Ask in business language. Never ask about XPath, microflows by technical name, or database schema.
  - Translate technical uncertainty to the business concern it represents.
  - Where helpful, offer concrete choices — but always allow "other" or "I don't know."
  - One focused question is better than three vague questions.

Prioritization factors:
  1. Blocking gaps (blocks_handoff: true) — always first
  2. Security/privacy uncertainty — elevated attention; HIGH when materially affecting authorization, privacy, or sensitive data
  3. Business rule / calculation ambiguity — high if financial or legal impact
  4. Role/data scope uncertainty — high when it affects access control
  5. Flow/validation gaps — medium; Business Flow structural gaps elevated to HIGH when
     the flow drives authorization, error handling, or multi-role handoff logic
  6. Visual/aesthetic preferences — low

---

## Business-Language Question Examples

Bad (technical):
  "What XPath constraint should be applied to the Manager entity access rule?"

Good (business):
  "Which records can a Department Manager see — only their own department, or all departments?"

Bad (technical):
  "Which ACT_ microflow implements the approval action?"

Good (business):
  "When a manager approves a request, what should happen? Should the requester receive a notification?"

Bad (assumes answer):
  "Since managers can create, they can also delete, right?"

Good (independent operation check):
  "Can managers delete records, or should they only be able to deactivate them?
   (Note: this is separate from their ability to create records.)"

Operations (READ, UPDATE, CREATE, DELETE, EXECUTE) are independently derived.
CANNOT UPDATE does not imply CANNOT READ. CAN READ does not imply CAN UPDATE.
Each operation's authorization must be confirmed or remain UNSPECIFIED.

Never use an overall percentage score as the authoritative maturity measure.
High visual completeness can hide critical security or business uncertainty.
Use dimension status + gaps + blockers + readiness gates instead.

---

## Unknown / Open Answer Handling

A Product Owner may say "I don't know" or provide no answer.

NEVER invent an answer for an unresolved business question.

Record the appropriate state:
  - "I don't know" → OPEN or UNKNOWN (depending on whether it is a gap or a capability)
  - "We haven't decided" → OPEN with status ASSUMPTION_REQUIRES_APPROVAL
  - "That's a risk we accept" → ACCEPTED_RISK with documented owner and consequence
  - "Someone else decides" → OPEN with owner field set

An unresolved question may remain in the mockup and the assessment.
Set blocking: true only when it materially affects handoff readiness.

Do not block the next mockup revision merely because questions remain open.
Honest uncertainty is more valuable than invented clarity.

---

## Provenance for Resolved Items

When a gap is resolved through the interview, record:
  - Resolution state: RESOLVED
  - Resulting decision/answer in the relevant Decision, Business Rule, or Assumption
  - Affected contract elements (which requirements, roles, rules were updated)
  - Revision in which it was resolved

Do not store full conversation transcripts in the contract.
Store the semantic outcome only.

## User-Facing Maturity Summary

When the user asks for a maturity summary or readiness review, present:

  Current state:
    [prototype_readiness] / [development_handoff_readiness]

  Strong:
    [dimensions with SUFFICIENT or STRONG status]

  Needs clarification:
    [dimensions with PARTIAL status — concise description of gap]

  Blocking issues (if any):
    [blocking gaps — plain language, not schema IDs]

  Highest-priority next topics:
    [top 3 items from next_exploration_areas]

Present in plain language. Do not surface raw GAP-NNN IDs or dimension IDs to the user
unless they specifically ask for them.

---

## Iteration Model

The lifecycle is iterative, not sequential:

  v0.x — Exploring: PROTOTYPE_READY, gaps visible, handoff not yet ready
  v0.x — Refining: key gaps addressed, critical dimensions improving
  v1.x — Validating: HANDOFF_READY candidate, final blockers resolved
  v1.x+ — Confirmed: HANDOFF_READY, ready for MxAgile Discovery

HTML is produced at every stage. Assessment metadata travels with the mockup.
A beautiful early mockup honestly marked as REFINEMENT_REQUIRED is the correct output.

## Risk-Based Exploration

Not all gaps are equal. Prioritization:

SECURITY_UNCLEAR or PRIVACY_UNCLEAR
  → Elevated assessment attention. HIGH priority and may block handoff when the unresolved
    issue materially affects authorization correctness, privacy, sensitive data, role isolation,
    data ownership, or active security boundaries. Not automatically HIGH or blocking merely
    because the gap category is security or privacy. Out-of-scope or non-material security
    gaps may legitimately be MEDIUM/LOW and non-blocking.

INCOMPLETE_DATA_SCOPE for security-relevant roles
  → HIGH priority when undefined data scope materially affects access control or authorization
    correctness (example: GAP-001 — data scope determines entity access rule scope and security
    test proof points). Not automatically HIGH merely because a role has a security label.
    Category alone does not determine blocks_handoff; assess material impact first.

MISSING_BUSINESS_RULE for financial/legal calculations
  → High priority. Wrong formula = wrong implementation.

MISSING_VALIDATION for required fields
  → Medium priority. Can often be resolved quickly.

MISSING_EDGE_CASE for rare scenarios
  → Low priority unless financial or security consequence.

ACCESSIBILITY_UNEXPLORED or NON_FUNCTIONAL_UNEXPLORED
  → Low priority for early iterations. Raise before final handoff.

Visual preference / label wording
  → Lowest priority. Do not block handoff for cosmetic detail.
